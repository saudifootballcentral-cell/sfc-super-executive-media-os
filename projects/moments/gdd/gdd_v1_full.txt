1. Executive Summary

“Moments” is a high-energy, physics-based local multiplayer party game designed to bring

people together in the same room. The game leverages an innovative “Phone-as-a-Controller”

architecture, allowing 2 to 8 players to instantly join the action using their smartphones without

needing to download an app. By combining the accessibility of Jackbox Games with the fast-

paced, skill-based chaos of Fall Guys or Mario Party, Moments delivers a seamless, zero-friction

multiplayer experience.

1.1. Core Design Pillars

1. Zero-Friction Onboarding: Players scan a QR code on the main screen to instantly join the
game via their phone’s web browser. No apps, no Bluetooth pairing, and no extra gamepads

are required.

2. Dynamic Adaptation: The phone’s user interface dynamically morphs to fit the mechanics
of the current mini-game (e.g., a steering wheel for racing, a dual-joystick for shooting).

3. Hero-Based Gameplay: Unlike typical party games where characters are purely cosmetic,
Moments features 8 distinct heroes with unique stats, abilities, and physics interactions,

adding a layer of strategic depth.

4. Procedural Polish: Heavy reliance on procedural visual effects (VFX) and dynamic audio

ensures the game looks and sounds like a AAA title while maintaining a minimal memory

footprint.

2. Game Flow & Session State

The game operates on a highly structured state machine managed by
SessionStateManager.cs , ensuring a robust flow even when players disconnect or
reconnect.

weapon ability, and a special ultimate ability.

4. The Mini-Games & Dynamic Hazards

Moments includes 7 fully playable mini-games, each inheriting from  MiniGameBase.cs . To
ensure matches never feel repetitive, the  ArenaHazardSystem  introduces dynamic, game-
altering events mid-match.

4.1. Polar Push (Survival)

Objective: Be the last player standing on a slippery, collapsing ice platform.

Mechanics: Players push each other using their bodies or abilities. The
IcePlatformManager  uses a Fisher-Yates shuffle to randomly select and drop outer ice
tiles over time, shrinking the arena.

Dynamic Hazard: Blizzard Gust — A sudden, massive wind force pushes all players in a

random direction. Players must fight the wind to avoid falling off.

4.2. Tank Battle (Combat)

Objective: Destroy all enemy tanks.

Mechanics: Twin-stick shooter mechanics. Cannonballs bounce off walls, requiring players

to calculate angles.

Dynamic Hazard: Artillery Strike — Red warning reticles appear on the ground. Two
seconds later, massive explosions deal heavy damage and knockback to anyone caught in

the blast radius.

4.3. Color Clash (Territory Control)

Objective: Paint the most floor tiles with your character’s color.

Mechanics: Walking over a neutral or enemy tile converts it to the player’s color. High-

speed characters (like Striker) have a distinct advantage here.

Dynamic Hazard: Paint Cannon — Massive paint blobs fall from the sky, instantly

overwriting large sections of the arena with random colors, forcing players to adapt their

routes.

4.4. Gravity Grab (Collection)

Objective: Collect floating orbs that match your color while avoiding incorrect ones.

Mechanics: The arena has reduced gravity, allowing for massive jumps and floaty

movement.

Dynamic Hazard: Gravity Inversion — Gravity suddenly reverses (pulling players toward
the ceiling) for 3 seconds, completely disrupting movement and strategy.

4.5. Bumper Blitz (Racing)

Objective: Be the first to complete 3 laps around the neon track.

Mechanics: Top-down racing with bumper-car physics. Players can ram each other into

walls or off the optimal racing line.

Dynamic Hazard: Electro Bumpers — Electrified zones spawn dynamically on the track.

Touching them stuns the player’s car for 2 seconds.

4.6. Blink Shot (Precision)

Objective: Shoot the most moving targets before time expires.

Mechanics: Players remain stationary but must aim quickly and precisely. Targets vary in
speed and point value.

Dynamic Hazard: EMP Pulse — An expanding electromagnetic ring sweeps the arena,

temporarily disabling all player weapons and abilities for 5 seconds.

4.7. Wave Rider (Balance & Survival)

Objective: Stay on your surfboard the longest.

Mechanics: The ocean is generated procedurally using Gerstner wave physics. Players
must jump over obstacles and balance their boards.

Dynamic Hazard: Rogue Wave — A massive, unavoidable wave sweeps across the screen,
applying immense upward force. Players must time their jumps perfectly to survive.

5. Smartphone Controller Architecture

The most critical technical achievement of Moments is the seamless integration of smartphones

as controllers, entirely bypassing the need for native apps or Bluetooth pairing.

5.1. Network Topology

The system utilizes a dual-server approach running locally on the host device (TV/PC):

1. HTTP Server ( PhoneControllerServer.cs ): Serves the HTML/CSS/JS frontend

( phone-controller.html ) to connecting phones.

2. WebSocket Server ( MomentsWebSocketServer.cs ): Handles real-time, bi-directional

communication between the phones and the Unity game engine.

5.2. Client-Side Prediction & Anti-Lag

To combat Wi-Fi latency and ensure joysticks feel responsive, the system implements Client-

Side Prediction:

The phone generates an  inputSequence  ID for every movement command sent.

The Unity host processes the movement and broadcasts a  StateSnapshotMsg  back to all
phones, including the  lastProcessedInputSequence .

The phone compares its current state with the server’s state and smoothly interpolates the

difference, eliminating visual stutter (rubber-banding) on the phone’s UI.

5.3. Dynamic UI Commands

The  ControllerGateway  sends  UICommand  messages to the phones to change their layout
based on the active phase or mini-game:

show_joystick : For games like Polar Push or Color Clash.

show_twin_stick : For Tank Battle.

show_steering : For Bumper Blitz.

show_hazard_warning : Triggers screen flashes and vibrations (via the HTML5 Vibration
API) on the phone when a dynamic hazard is about to occur.

6. Art Direction & Procedural Visuals

Moments employs a vibrant, stylized art direction optimized for high frame rates (60 FPS) on
smart TVs and lower-end hardware. The Universal Render Pipeline (URP) is heavily utilized to

achieve a polished look without the performance overhead of traditional AAA pipelines.

6.1. Procedural VFX System

Instead of relying entirely on heavy particle system prefabs, Moments uses a highly optimized
VFXManager  that generates effects procedurally via coroutines.

Electric Arcs ( FX_ElectricArc ): Used for eliminations and EMP hazards. Lines are
drawn dynamically using  LineRenderer  with randomized offsets to simulate electricity.

Speed Streaks ( FX_SpeedStreak ): Trail renderers attached to characters during dashes,
dynamically colored based on the player’s assigned color.

Hit Sparks ( FX_HitSpark ): Fast, expanding spheres that quickly fade alpha to simulate
impacts, avoiding the overdraw of complex particle systems.

6.2. Dynamic Arena Environments

The  ArenaEnvironment  script dynamically transforms the base lighting and background
elements based on the active mini-game, creating a distinct mood for each round:

Polar Push: Cool blue ambient light, dense fog, and animated Aurora Borealis directional
lights sweeping across the sky.

Color Clash: Dark ambient lighting pierced by rotating neon spotlights and glowing pipes.

Tank Battle: Warm, sandy fog with a harsh directional “Desert Sun” and a subtle heat-haze
effect applied via a post-processing distortion material.

7. Audio Design & Spatial Sound

Audio in Moments is designed to provide immediate, satisfying feedback and enhance the
chaotic party atmosphere. The  AudioManager  acts as a central hub for all sound events.

7.1. Dynamic Soundscapes

Each mini-game loads a specific ambient track that loops continuously, setting the scene before
the music even begins (e.g., howling arctic winds for Polar Push, factory machinery hums for

Color Clash).

7.2. Procedural & Spatial SFX

Sound effects are heavily spatialized. When a tank fires a cannonball, the sound originates from

the tank’s 3D position, allowing players to instinctively react to threats off-screen. The system
also supports procedural pitch-shifting to ensure repetitive sounds (like footsteps or rapid
gunfire) do not cause ear fatigue.

8. Technical Architecture & Performance

The game is built with scalability and long-term stability in mind, addressing common pitfalls
found in local multiplayer games.

8.1. State Management & Null Safety

The  SessionStateManager  is the single source of truth. By strictly controlling the  Phase
enum, the game prevents race conditions (e.g., a player attempting to join while a mini-game is
actively loading). Extensive null-checking and list-copying ( new List<>(players) ) are
employed throughout the codebase to prevent  ConcurrentModificationException  errors
when iterating over player lists during rapid join/leave events.

8.2. Garbage Collection (GC) Optimization

To maintain a stable 60 FPS, GC allocations in Update loops have been aggressively minimized.

Caching: Dictionaries and lists used for scoring or physics calculations are cached and
cleared rather than instantiated newly every frame.

Network Throttling: Instead of broadcasting the game timer to phones every frame,
updates are throttled and synchronized only at specific intervals (e.g., every 5 seconds or
during critical events), massively reducing network overhead.

9. Future Expansion & Roadmap

The architecture of Moments is designed to be easily expandable. Adding new content requires
minimal code refactoring due to the data-driven approach.

9.1. Content Expansion

New Characters: Created simply by duplicating a  CharacterDefinition
ScriptableObject, assigning a new 3D model, and selecting abilities from the existing
CharacterAbilitySystem  dropdowns.

New Mini-Games: Implemented by inheriting from  MiniGameBase.cs . The core loop,
scoring, and UI transitions are handled automatically by the base class.

9.2. Planned Features (v2.0)

1. Character Mastery (XP System): A progression system where playing specific characters

unlocks cosmetic upgrades (e.g., High-Fidelity models) at Level 10.

2. In-Game Shop: A marketplace accessible directly from the phone controller while waiting in

the lobby, allowing players to purchase horizontal cosmetics (hats, custom VFX) using
earned in-game currency.

3. Cloud Save Persistence: Transitioning player profiles from local  PlayerPrefs  to a cloud

database to retain progression across different host devices.
