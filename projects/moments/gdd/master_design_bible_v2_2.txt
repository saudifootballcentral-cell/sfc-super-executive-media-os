Executive Overview
Moments is a couch-first party game system in which the TV hosts the shared session and each player joins on a phone by scanning a QR code. The TV provides spectacle, camera, scoreboards, voting, and mini-game state. Phones provide zero-friction joining, character selection, ready confirmation, and context-specific controller layouts for each mini-game.
• Core promise: anyone in the room can join in seconds without needing a traditional gamepad.
• Product shape: one main game hub called Moments containing a set of fast, readable, stylized 3D mini-games.
• Technology direction: Unity on the TV / host side, lightweight mobile web controller or companion app on the phone side.
• Design rule: characters are cosmetic-first, not balance-defining, so the roster adds identity without unfair advantages.

1. Product Vision and Experience Pillars
Moments should feel like a premium local multiplayer party game: instant to join, easy to read from the couch, funny even when players are losing, and energetic enough to turn a living room or event space into a shared showpiece. Every mini-game is framed as a vivid 3D "moment" - a tiny social challenge blown up into a toy-like arena.

Experience Pillars
• Instant social entry: The room should convert from watching to playing in under ten seconds per person.
• Readable chaos: Even at peak action the audience should understand who is winning, who got hit, and what the objective is.
• Short-form variety: A full playlist should rotate through different mechanics so the game never feels like one repeated input pattern.
• Character attachment: Players should quickly identify with a hero, color, and set of emotes that carry across the full session.
• TV-first spectacle: The television is the center of gravity - hero shots, results screens, podiums, and reactions must feel celebratory.
Core Design Rules
• Characters do not alter balance in the launch version; they are cosmetic-first.
• Every mini-game must teach its controls in a few seconds through TV instruction cards plus simplified phone input screens.
• Any important state that affects the room - score, readiness, timer, elimination, vote result - belongs on the TV, not hidden only on phones.
• Phone screens should never require dense text or multi-step interaction during active play.

Scene flow principle

3. TV-as-Server System Architecture
The TV host runs the authoritative game session. It owns room creation, player slots, mini-game state, score aggregation, and scene orchestration. Phones do not simulate the full game. They connect to the active room, receive local UI state, and send compact input messages.

Authority Model
• The TV generates a unique room token and encodes it into the QR join URL.
• Phones authenticate into the room with that token and receive an assigned player slot or a queued state if the room is full.
• During gameplay, phones send intent only: move vector, tap, hold, steer, aim, fire, dash, or vote selection.
• The TV validates and resolves all gameplay consequences, then broadcasts the resulting state to the room.
• On disconnect, the host reserves the slot for a short grace window so the same player can reconnect without losing their character or score.

4. QR Join, Lobby, and Character Select
The join funnel must be fast enough that a new player can enter the room without instructions from the host. For the first playable, the fastest route is a large QR code on the TV that opens a lightweight web controller with the session token already embedded.
Recommended Join Funnel
• Scan QR on the TV.
• Phone opens the controller page and validates the active room token.
• Player enters a nickname or accepts an auto-generated one.
• Player browses the pre-designed hero roster, previews, and locks a character.
• Player presses Ready; the TV updates the lobby card in real time.
• When everyone is ready, the room advances to countdown or game voting.

Character selection rule set
Launch with a curated roster of pre-designed heroes. Duplicates are off by default, but the host may enable duplicates in party settings. Locked characters remain reserved until the player unlocks, disconnects beyond the grace period, or the session resets.

Character Production Specification
• Create a CharacterDefinition ScriptableObject with hero id, display name, one-line fantasy, portrait sprite, 3D prefab reference, voice pack, emote list, color tag, and availability state.
• Design each hero with a large head, simplified torso, strong hands, and one signature prop so they remain readable at TV distance.
• Share a common rig and animation vocabulary across the roster to keep Unity production efficient.
• Keep material response premium and toy-like: clean roughness values, soft rim lighting, and broad value separation between skin, cloth, metal, and prop surfaces.
• Author front, side, and back turnarounds before final in-engine modeling to protect silhouette clarity.

Controller Layout Rules
• Never show more than two primary thumb zones during active play unless a mini-game truly requires it.
• Swap layouts dynamically per mini-game so the phone only presents relevant controls.
• Use haptic pulses for join confirm, ready confirm, impact hits, item pickup, elimination, and podium placement.
• Frame the phone UI with strong player color so the controller itself reinforces identity in the room.
7. TV UX, Phone UX, and Wireframe Notes
This section consolidates the earlier wireframe pack into implementation guidance so the master document stays readable without carrying large page-screenshot inserts.
The television is the public stage; the phone is private utility. That separation should remain consistent throughout the session.
TV UX Principles
• Large type and low clutter for readability from 2 to 5 meters away.
• QR code remains visible in the join state and may stay anchored in one corner while players are still entering.
• Character lineup and readiness should be legible in a single glance.
• Results screens should favor celebration and comparison over dense statistics.
Phone UX Principles
• Join flow is linear: Scan -&gt; Name -&gt; Character Select -&gt; Ready.
• Character cards must use large portraits, one-line fantasy copy, and clear available / taken / locked states.
• During gameplay, the screen should be dark and battery-safe, with only the control zones and essential prompts visible.
• Disconnected users should see a clear reconnect state rather than a silent failure screen.

9. First Playable Scope and Production Roadmap
For fastest validation, the first milestone should prove the social funnel, one complete lobby loop, and a small set of mini-games that collectively test the core control patterns.

7 mini-games, audio pass, balancing, accessibility, localization

Launch Readiness
Optimization, QA matrix, marketing capture, storefront assets, live-ops hooks
Supports real-world deployment quality.
Recommended First Three Mini-Games
• Polar Push - proves movement plus dash with a universally readable objective.
• Color Clash - proves territory capture and strong TV readability of floor-state changes.
• Tank Battle - proves steering, firing, and more advanced phone input mapping.
Production focus
Do not overbuild customization before the join loop, TV readability, and controller reliability are solid. The fastest way to make Moments feel premium is a flawless start-to-finish room flow plus one or two extremely polished mini-games.

Implementation Notes
• Keep all gameplay-affecting state authoritative on the TV. Phones should not own match simulation.
• Use Addressables for mini-game scenes so the shell stays stable while content scales.
• Cache the phone web app aggressively after first launch to reduce repeat join friction.
• Make controller layout assignment data-driven, not hard-coded per screen, so new mini-games can be added without rebuilding the full UI layer.
• Design all TV screens with couch-distance testing at real scale, not just in the Unity Game view on a desktop monitor.
