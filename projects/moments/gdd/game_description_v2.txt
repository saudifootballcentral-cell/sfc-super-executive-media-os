1. Game Overview
Moments is a fast-paced, highly interactive local multiplayer party game played on a shared screen (TV, PC, or
Console) while players use their own smartphones as controllers. It eliminates the need for extra gamepads,
making it instantly accessible for groups of 2 to 8 players — with AI bots filling empty slots so even two players
experience full-room chaos.

Version 2.0 expands the game from 7 to 12 mini-games, layers a universal power-up framework, team and
tournament modes, a progression system (Character Mastery and the seasonal Party Pass), and a
presentation overhaul — dynamic camera direction, kill-cam replays, a bilingual announcer (Arabic / English), and
a consistent hazard-telegraph language — bringing the experience to a professional, commercially competitive
standard.

2. Core Gameplay Loop
The Moments experience is divided into five phases, managed seamlessly by the SessionStateManager:

(cid:127) Attract Mode: The game idles on a visually stunning screen displaying a QR code. Players scan it to instantly

join via their phone's web browser.

(cid:127) Lobby & Character Selection: Players enter nicknames and select heroes from a carousel on their phones;

3D avatars spawn on the TV. The host chooses a session format and optional mutators.

(cid:127) Mini-Game Roulette: The system selects a mini-game (random or playlist). A brief 'How to Play' screen

explains the rules.

(cid:127) Action Phase: Core gameplay with power-ups, dynamic hazards, and adaptive music. Eliminated players stay

engaged through phone spectator mode and Crowd Chaos hazard voting.

(cid:127) Results & Podium: A kill-cam highlight replay, a Bonus Awards ceremony (Most Aggressive, Survivor,

Comeback King...), then scores. After the final round, the champion is crowned on a celebratory podium with
per-character victory choreography and a shareable result card.

Session Formats: Quick Party (3 games), Full Party (7), Marathon (all 12), Gauntlet (best-of-5 on one game),
Team Mode (2v2 / 4v4), and Tournament Bracket (8 players, semifinals to final). The final round is always worth
double points so no one is ever out of the race.

3. The Heroes (Character Roster)

passive stats, a dash, a weapon attack, and a game-changing special ability. With v2.0, each hero also earns
Mastery XP, unlocking skins, trails, victory poses, and announcer lines up to a high-fidelity model at Level 10.

4. The Mini-Games (12 Arenas of Chaos)
Each mini-game ships with multiple arena variants, at least two dynamic hazards driven by the
ArenaHazardSystem, full power-up support, bot support, team scoring, and its own announcer lines and adaptive
music layer.

4.1 Polar Push — Survival

Objective: Be the last player standing on a shrinking, slippery ice platform.

Mechanics: Players push each other off the edge while ice tiles crack and collapse in three readable stages (solid,
cracked, breaking).

v2.0 Features:

(cid:127) Grab & Throw: grapple a nearby opponent and hurl them; victims mash to escape.

(cid:127) Power-up crates: Anchor Boots (knockback immunity), Ice Pick (crack a tile under an enemy), Snowball

Launcher.

(cid:127) Final Duel: with 2 players left, the arena shrinks to one platform with a camera zoom and slow-mo.

(cid:127) Arena variants: Glacier Ring (donut), Iceberg Tilt (weight-based tilting), Frozen Lake (standing still breaks the

ice).

Dynamic Hazards:

(cid:127) Blizzard Gust — wind pushes everyone in a random direction.

(cid:127) Orca Breach — a whale bursts through, destroying tiles permanently.

(cid:127) Freezing Fog — a slowing fog wall; caught players see frost on their phone screens.

4.2 Tank Battle — Combat

Objective: Destroy all enemy tanks to be the last survivor (or top the kill-streak board in Respawn Brawl).

Mechanics: Twin-stick shooter with bouncing cannonballs and destructible, three-stage cover.

v2.0 Features:

(cid:127) Shell pickups: Bouncer (3 ricochets), Heavy (destroys cover), Cluster (splits in 3), Homing.

(cid:127) Respawn Brawl variant: 90-second deathmatch with 3-second respawns — eliminated players never sit idle.

(cid:127) Hero tank traits: passives carry into tank stats.

(cid:127) Arena variants: Urban Ruins (ricochet corridors), Open Dunes, Factory (conveyor floors).

Dynamic Hazards:

(cid:127) Artillery Strike — warning reticles, then devastating explosions.

(cid:127) Sandstorm — visibility shrinks to a radius; the phone minimap becomes vital.

(cid:127) Supply Bombardment — shells and loot crates fall together: risk versus reward.

4.3 Color Clash — Territory Control

Objective: Paint the most floor tiles with your color before time runs out.

Mechanics: Walking paints tiles; enclosing a full shape fills its interior instantly, rewarding clever routes over raw
speed.

v2.0 Features:

(cid:127) Slip Streams: +15% speed on your color, -10% on enemy paint.

(cid:127) Paint Roller power-up triples trail width; every player carries 2 throwable Paint Bombs.

(cid:127) Team Mode: shared team colors in 2v2v2v2 or 4v4.

Dynamic Hazards:

(cid:127) Paint Cannon — giant blobs overwrite whole sections.

5. Universal Gameplay Systems
Power-Up Framework: a shared, data-driven pickup system inherited by every mini-game. A spawn director
subtly biases drops toward trailing players, and the phone shows the held power-up as a large tappable button.

Comeback & Award Design: double-points final round, an optional Underdog Boost, and a Bonus Awards
ceremony before the podium — Most Aggressive, Survivor, Comeback King, Pacifist — each worth points and
tracked automatically by the MatchStatsRecorder.

Mutators: host-selectable chaos toggles — Tiny Mode, Double Speed, Inverted Hazards, hazard intensity (Off /
Normal / Chaos) — for endless replayability.

AI Bots: three difficulty levels fill empty slots through the same input API as phones, also powering automated
soak-testing.

6. Smartphone Controller 2.0
The standout feature of Moments remains its seamless Phone-as-a-Controller technology — no app, no pairing,
just a QR scan — now expanded into a platform of its own:

(cid:127) Dynamic layouts: the phone UI morphs per mini-game — steering wheel with drift paddle, twin-stick, gyro

pointer, rhythm pads.

(cid:127) Motion controls: gyroscope aiming (Blink Shot), tilt steering (Bumper Blitz), lean balance (Wave Rider), with

one-tap calibration.

(cid:127) Haptic language: distinct vibration grammar — hazard warning (3 short), elimination (long), power-up ready

(double tap), hidden bomb timer pulses.

(cid:127) Private information screens: your true map during Disco Floor, your secret role in Heist Run, your bomb timer

in Bomb Relay — gameplay impossible on shared-screen gamepads.

(cid:127) Emotes & soundboard: rate-limited taunts and character voice lines triggered from the phone.

(cid:127) Spectator & Crowd Chaos: eliminated players watch a minimap and collectively vote one extra hazard per

round.

(cid:127) Zero-latency networking: custom C# WebSocket server with client-side prediction delivers sub-50ms local

latency; a 10-second AI-takeover grace period covers disconnects.

(cid:127) Controller accessibility: button scaling, left-handed layouts, high-contrast skin, reduced-vibration toggle.

7. Presentation & Game Feel

(cid:127) Dynamic Camera Director: automatic zooms and pans, slow-mo on final eliminations.

(cid:127) Kill-Cam Replays: a 5-second highlight of each round's decisive moment.

(cid:127) Announcer System: procedural commentary with anti-repetition logic, shipping with Arabic and English voice

packs.

(cid:127) Crowd audio, hit-stop and screen shake: dynamic cheering that swells with the action, plus 2–4 frame impact

freezes.

(cid:127) Adaptive music: intensity layers stack as timers run down or a final duel begins.

(cid:127) Telegraph standard: every hazard follows the same grammar — phone vibration + flash, a 2-second TV

warning, then the event. Consistency means fairness.

(cid:127) Victory choreography: per-character podium animations, confetti and fireworks — and a shareable photo-card

of the results.

8. Progression & Retention
Character Mastery: per-hero XP unlocks skins, trail colors, victory poses and announcer lines, with a high-fidelity
model at Level 10. Party Pass: a seasonal, free cosmetic track tied to matches played — strictly no pay-to-win.
Player profiles persist stats (wins, favorite hero, nemesis) locally with planned cloud sync, and an achievement
system surfaces challenges on the phone. The auto-generated share card after every session doubles as built-in
social marketing.

9. Technical Pillars

(cid:127) Unity 6000+ / URP tuned for a stable 60 FPS on smart TVs and modest hardware.

(cid:127) Procedural visuals: VFXManager and PostProcessingManager generate effects dynamically — electric arcs,

speed streaks, hit sparks — with minimal memory overhead.

(cid:127) Dynamic audio: spatial 3D sound, procedural pitch-shifting, per-arena ambient scapes.

(cid:127) Robust state management: SessionStateManager and ControllerGateway prevent race conditions across

joins, disconnects, and reconnections; GC allocations in update loops are aggressively minimized and network
updates throttled.

(cid:127) Data-driven content: heroes, hazards, power-ups, and mutators are ScriptableObjects — new content without

code refactoring.
